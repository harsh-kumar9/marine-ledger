# How to investigate a disagreement

A difference between two readings earns a closer look when choosing between them changes a scientific claim. Agreement can also earn a check when two methods used the same pixels, coordinates or catalog. Preserve the original readings, propose at least two interpretations, then identify a measurement that could distinguish them. Record the outcome even when a familiar explanation wins.

## When a photograph adds a missing station

The taxonomic registry and the museum answer different questions at station 164. WoRMS maps the historical species name *Triton gracilis* Reeve to *Reticutriton pfeifferianus*. The Naturalis specimen record RMNH.MOL.193497 prefers *Monoplex vespaceus*. Its label reads “Halmaheira zee” and station 164. The 1909 report lists four shells under *gracilis* at that station, while the museum lot contains one. The label establishes a shared expedition, place, station and historical name. Discriminating between present-day identifications requires the specimen and its determination history, and linking it to one of the four printed shells requires accession evidence. The two names and both counts belong in the record. [Printed account](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/2205139) · [Museum specimen](https://data.biodiversitydata.nl/naturalis/specimen/RMNH.MOL.193497) · [Photographed label](https://medialib.naturalis.nl/file/id/RMNH.MOL.193497/format/large)

A second photographed label makes a stronger event link. RMNH.MOL.193059 records the *Siboga*, Haingsisi on Samau, station 60 and the old variety *orientalis*. Schepman lists one shell there under that variety. The initial aggregated catalog searches missed this link. A museum search followed by a visual reading of the label recovered it. Agreement on material and count supports the association, while the museum lot identifier remains the appropriate citation. [Printed account](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/2205140) · [Museum specimen](https://data.biodiversitydata.nl/naturalis/specimen/RMNH.MOL.193059) · [Photographed label](https://medialib.naturalis.nl/file/id/RMNH.MOL.193059/format/large)

After recovering five museum vouchers, a search with each exact Naturalis provider identifier returned five GBIF occurrence records. All five lacked indexed event date, field number and event ID. The contrast with the zero-result species-and-expedition query identifies a metadata retrieval failure. It preserves credit for specimens that were already catalogued and gives the project five positive controls for future query changes. [Museum-to-GBIF crosswalk](catalog/museum.json) · [Station 60 in GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2444503155) · [Station 164 in GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2444503569)

The station 60 specimen introduces a separate measurement discrepancy. The ticket says 36 m. The ship’s register lists 23 m and mentions shallow algal habitat. Schepman’s station 60 row gives no numeric depth. Keep the voucher association and withhold a single specimen depth pending recovery of the haul log.
